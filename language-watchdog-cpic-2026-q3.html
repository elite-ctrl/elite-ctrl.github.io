Three Places Where the English of CPIC’s 2026 Q3 Report Parts Company with the Chinese

Language Watchdog — a desk note on where the English parallel parts company with the Chinese filing.

Framing

China Pacific Insurance’s 2026 third-quarter report was filed in both Chinese and English on 28 October. For A+H insurers the Chinese text is usually the original, written to the Shanghai Stock Exchange template; the English is the parallel version. Looking at the two side by side turns up three spots where the English says something different from the Chinese.

The English filing is available here: HKEX filing (CPIC 2026 Q3)

Coverage counted as policies instead of person-times

Source (TC)

普惠覆蓋人群超2.8億人次

Target (EN)

a total of over 280 million inclusive insurance policies were issued

The Chinese counts person-times of inclusive-insurance coverage. The English turns this into a statement about policies issued. Person-times and policies issued are not the same thing. One counts how many times people were covered; the other counts contracts. The English invents a policy figure that is not in the Chinese and drops the coverage figure that is.

This is not a close rephrasing. The distance between source and target is too wide to be described as “smoothing for English readers.” Smoothing would keep the same fact while adjusting the phrasing. Here the English replaces the disclosed metric (coverage in person-times) with a different metric (policies issued) that the Chinese does not contain. It is a substitution, not an adaptation.

HKEX English filings by other PRC insurers treat 人次 as a distinct metric and commonly render it as “person-times.” PICC sustainability and annual reports, for example, use “served 1.68 billion person-times” and “providing coverage for over 1.5 billion person-times.” China Life English versions likewise retain “person-times” for coverage and claims-service counts. The term is established in this disclosure genre. A source-locked rendering would be:

inclusive insurance coverage exceeded 280 million person-times

or

inclusive insurance programmes covered more than 280 million person-times

“Policies were issued” should not be used unless the Chinese text itself states 保單 or 承保保單.

This is the strongest of the three differences: an English-only reader is given a different metric. If the inclusive-finance volume figure is material to how the company presents its activity, this is the one that most clearly warrants a clarification.

“Confirming to” instead of “conforming to”

Source (TC)

其他符合非經常性損益定義的損益項目

Target (EN)

Other items confirming to the definition of non-recurring profit or loss

The Chinese has items that 符合 (meet / conform to) the definition of non-recurring profit or loss. The English says “items confirming to the definition.” “Confirming to” is not the right English here. The normal phrase is “conforming to” or “that meet the definition of.” It is a small word, but it sits on a line people use to strip out one-offs and look at recurring earnings. Getting the verb wrong makes the label less precise than the Chinese. The meaning is still recoverable, however, so this collocation error is unlikely to justify a formal clarification on its own.

The enquiry-and-confirmation step disappears

Source (TC)

經本公司詢問並經相關股東確認，除此之外，本公司未獲知上述股東存在其他關聯關係或一致行動關係。

Target (EN)

Other than this, the Company is not aware of any related relations or concerted actions among the above-mentioned shareholders.

The Chinese says the Company asked the relevant shareholders and they confirmed there were no other related-party or concert-party relationships, and only then states that the Company is not aware of any others. The English keeps only the second half. The diligence step—enquiry by the Company and confirmation by the shareholders—is gone. That sentence is a standard piece of quarterly disclosure. Leaving it out makes the English version a weaker statement than the Chinese one. The core negative representation remains, so the omission is a legitimate proofreading flag and a borderline candidate for clarification, but it is weaker than the metric substitution.

Closing

None of these is just a style choice. Each one changes what an English-only reader can take from the document relative to the Chinese original. When the English is a translation of a template-driven Chinese report, the practical test is whether the same facts, the same metrics and the same representations survive. In these three places they do not. Of the three, only the person-times / policies substitution rises to the level of a clarification in ordinary practice. The other two are real source–target asymmetries that should be corrected in the English text, but they do not, on their own, warrant a clarifying announcement. The person-times case is the clearest illustration: the gap is not a matter of English readability; it is a change of the fact disclosed.
