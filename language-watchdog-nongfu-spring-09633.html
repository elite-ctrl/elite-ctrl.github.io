Nongfu Spring (09633): when fluent English changes the disclosure

Language Watchdog — Chinese source, English parallel, three slips where fluent English changes the event, the roles, or the claim.

Framing

Translation accuracy depends on the event a sentence describes, the roles it assigns, and the scope of its qualifiers.

In our experience of Hong Kong financial translation, issuer teams and production suppliers like us often compare both language versions. Readers may rely on just one. For them, a shift in meaning may pass unnoticed: that version is the disclosure through which they understand the issuer’s position. A change in an action, a metric, or the strength of a claim can shape that understanding.

Nongfu Spring (09633)’s 2024 annual results announcement, released on 25 March 2025, provides three examples. For this discussion, I treat Chinese as the source and assess the published English against it; the translation direction is inferred, not established.

Public filing — Nongfu Spring (09633): EN · TC.

1. Predicate specificity — p. 19

The first concerns predicate specificity. The Chinese states that 「相關行政執法部門依法對部分造謠網絡賬號進行了處置」. Its predicate, 「進行了處置」, reports action taken against certain accounts without specifying the precise procedure. The English describes “certain rumor-mongering accounts prosecuted by the appropriate administrative law-enforcement agencies”.

Source (TC)

相關行政執法部門依法對部分造謠網絡賬號進行了處置

Target (EN)

certain rumor-mongering accounts prosecuted by the appropriate administrative law-enforcement agencies

For an English-language reader, “prosecuted” may evoke formal legal proceedings and their associated seriousness. 「進行了處置」 tells a Chinese reader that action was taken without specifying the procedure. Each version may leave a different impression.

The passage separately mentions proceedings against certain responsible entities; it does not establish that the action against the accounts was prosecution. The distinction matters across legal contexts.

Fix: “the relevant administrative law-enforcement agencies took action against certain rumor-spreading online accounts in accordance with law”.

Why it matters: An unsupported claim of prosecution can misstate the nature of enforcement action in a listed issuer’s disclosure.

2. Event roles and measurement — p. 21

The second concerns event roles and measurement. For the 「茶π上新」 exhibition, the Chinese reports 「綫下覆蓋人數超過500萬人」. The English says “more than 5 million people participating”.

Source (TC)

開展「茶π上新」主題巡迴展，綫下覆蓋人數超過500萬人

Target (EN)

the ‘Tea π New Arrival (茶π上新)’ themed traveling exhibition … with more than 5 million people participating

The number is preserved, but people counted as reached become people described as participants. The English also omits “offline”. Reach and participation need not measure the same engagement; the Chinese does not establish that everyone reached took part.

Fix: “the ‘Tea π New Arrival (茶π上新)’ themed traveling exhibition … reaching more than 5 million people offline”.

Why it matters: Reach and participation measure different levels of engagement, so conflating them can overstate the outcome of an issuer’s marketing activity.

3. Scope of negation — p. 23

The third concerns the scope of negation. In its discussion of citrus disease research, the Chinese says 「這些物質至今仍無法用儀器完全區分」; the English says the compounds “remain indistinguishable by instruments”.

Source (TC)

這些物質至今仍無法用儀器完全區分，但狗的鼻子卻能嗅到其中的差異

Target (EN)

While these compounds remain indistinguishable by instruments, dogs can detect the difference.

「無法……完全區分」 denies complete distinction. “Indistinguishable” is stronger: it presents the compounds as not distinguishable. The Chinese establishes neither complete indistinguishability nor the extent of any partial distinction.

Fix: “Instruments still cannot fully distinguish these substances, whereas dogs can detect the differences.”

Why it matters: An absolute claim can overstate a technical limitation in the issuer’s account of its research.

The Takeaway

Across the three examples, fluent English is not the decisive test. A source-first review asks whether the translation preserves the same event, the same roles, and the same limits on what is being asserted.
